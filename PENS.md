My goal is to create a wooden pen that I actually like to use.

I don't necessarily need to make something fully custom and bespoke. It just seems like I have to go that way because I can't find exactly what I'm looking for. This is why I wind up 3D printing specific pieces but if I can find parts on sites like pennstateind.com, then I'd be more than happy to purchase them.

A 3D-printed nose cannot fit a 7 mm tube and remain strong enough, regardless of the ink cartridge. A 7 mm build requires a metal nose.



## Things I do not like
- Cross style refills
- Ballpoint pens
- Thick pens
    - Pens that start with barrels that are 3/8", 9mm, or larger
- Wooden pens where your fingers spend their entire time resting on plastic or metal. Why bother with a wooden pen at that point?

## Things I do like
- Pens with needle or conical points
- 0.5mm or smaller points
- A variety of ink colors
- Pens where your fingers spend most of their time resting against wood. That just feels so nice.
- Pilot brand ink cartridges in general. This doesn't limit me to only Pilot, but I do tend to like their pens.
- I have several Pilot Precise V5 RT refills on hand, so I would prefer to use them for now.

## Future options

- Consider a closed-end wooden pen using PSI's Handle Anything Chuck System (PKHASET1). An 8 mm tube also needs the PKTC06 mandrel shaft and PKTC08 threaded plug. The plug stays in the tube after turning but is not used by the finished pen; allow for the space it occupies. A top-click mechanism would need an opening at the back, so this approach is better suited to a different mechanism.
